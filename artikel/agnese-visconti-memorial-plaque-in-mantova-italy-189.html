📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Within the sprawling grounds of Mantua's Ducal Palace lies Piazza Pallone (now officially Piazza Lega Lombarda), a quiet, tree-lined square, few tourists notice the simple stone plaque mounted on the perimeter wall enclosing the space.

The plaque commemorates one of the darkest episodes of Mantuan Renaissance history: the beheading of Agnese Visconti on February 7, 1391.

Agnese, daughter of the Lord of Milan, Bernabò Visconti, was married at just fourteen years old to Francesco I Gonzaga, Lord of Mantua. Twelve years later, her husband accused her of adultery with the knight Antonio da Scandiano (also known as Vincenzo da Scalona). Her alleged lover was hanged in the square, while Agnese was beheaded at the age of 28 in the very same spot.

Historians generally agree that the accusation was a political setup orchestrated by Francesco I Gonzaga to sever ties with the Visconti family and forge a latest alliance with the Republic of Venice. The bodies of both executed lovers were buried without ceremony in unconsecrated ground within the square.

Over the centuries, the injustice suffered by Agnese seemed to permeate the stones of the square, giving rise to one of Lombardy's most famous ghost stories.

It is remarked that on cold winter nights—particularly around the anniversary of her death in February—when dense fog from the Mincio River rolls over the palace courtyards, the shadowy figure of Agnese reappears near the plaque.

Industry observers note that local lore claims her headless spirit wanders desperately around the perimeter of the square searching for her severed head or clutching a stained shroud to her chest. Over the years, passersby have reported hearing heavy footsteps on the ground, the rustle of period garments, and a quiet weeping that cuts off abruptly right in front of the stone tablet.

Funnyly the XIX century plaque lists an incorrect age for the noblewoman at the time of her execution—a historical error preserved on the marble during restorations.

The plaque is located along the wall delimiting Piazza Pallone, adjacent to the Ducal Palace complex and just a short walk from Piazza Sordello.
