According to recent reports, air India and Air India Express now both have latest CEOs, and the group’s transformation looks steadier for it. The harder test is whether the new leaders can cut losses that are still running in the billions.

In a recent development, air India Express on Wednesday announced the appointment of its chief financial officer Deepak Rajawat as CEO, effective Dec. 1, placing a finance and commercial executive at the helm as the airline enters a more demanding phase of its growth under Tata Group, which controls the Air India Group.

Rajawat succeeds Aloke Singh, who left in March to join rival IndiGo as chief strategy officer. Singh oversaw the fleet growing from 26 planes to more than 100, and its merger with AirAsia India also took place on his watch.

The new CEO brings more than two decades of aviation experience, including a stint as chief commercial officer at Vistara, the Tata-Singapore Airlines joint venture that merged into Air India.
