Sources indicate that A 12.6% drop from Asia fueled the seventh straight month of U.S. inbound travel declines, more evidence that the U.S. is losing ground as other countries compete for the same long-haul travelers.

Sharp declines from Asia and Western Europe fueled the seventh consecutive month of declining overseas tourism into the United States, according to preliminary figures from the National Travel and Tourism Office released on Tuesday.

About 2.8 million overseas visitors entered the United States in October, down 7.2% from last year.

Much of last month’s decline stems from a 12.6% dip in travelers from Asia, the largest source region outside of Western Europe. South Korean arrivals were down 37.9%, Japan was down 10.3%, China down 2.3%, and India down 1%.
