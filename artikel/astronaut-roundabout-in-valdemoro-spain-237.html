Astronaut Roundabout in Valdemoro, Spain

Valdemoro, a town south of Madrid better known for its proximity to Pinto than for space exploration, has an unlikely visitor from another world standing in the middle of one of its roundabouts. Francisco Leiro’s El Astronauta, installed in 2001, has become one of the town’s most distinctive modern landmarks. The four-meter-tall stainless-steel figure appears to be taking a monumental step across the surface of the Moon—or perhaps simply trying to cross the road.

Sources indicate that the astronaut stands on a large hemisphere designed to resemble the lunar surface. Originally conceived for the Rotonda de los Tritones, the structure was covered with dark mineral material to create the illusion of a fragment of the Moon. Today the location is known as the Glorieta del Universo, an appropriately cosmic name for a traffic circle whose principal resident has clearly travelled much farther than most of its commuters.

In a recent development, the sculpture is considerably more substantial than its whimsical subject might suggest. It weighs around 2,600 kilograms and was cast in stainless steel at Fundición Capa in nearby Arganda del Rey. The complicated process involved casting the work in around ten separate pieces before assembling, welding, and polishing them.

According to recent reports, leiro’s astronaut was intended to symbolize progress, the future, and humanity’s desire to overcome its limits. More than two decades later, it has achieved something perhaps more difficult: becoming a beloved emblem of Valdemoro. Against the otherwise empty roundabout, the gleaming figure has plenty of space to contemplate the universe—and plenty of passing drivers to contemplate him.
