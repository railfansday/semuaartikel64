Unlike most surviving Cham towers that crown dramatic hilltops, Binh Lam Tower rises almost unexpectedly from the flat, verdant plains of a quiet lowland village.

Constructed around the late tenth to early eleventh century, this 20-meter brick sanctuary serves as a vital architectural bridge, catching the art form mid-evolution between the delicate, lyrical features of the My Son A1 style and the grand, spear-like forms of classic Binh Dinh towers.

Its weathered facade features slender vertical pilasters, mini-temple false doors, and faint relief carvings of the mythical bird-god Garuda. Beyond its standing brickwork, Binh Lam hides deep subterranean secrets: archaeological digs uncovered vast foundations of a once-extensive religious complex, alongside stone Nagas and relief sculptures.

Furthermore, the tower once formed the eastern heart of ancient Thi Nai Citadel, positioned along river channels leading to the historical port—making this lowland monument a crucial physical anchor to Champa's lost naval and architectural empire.

Getting there: Accessible throughout the day, with soft morning light ideal for inspecting the intricate brickwork details and false-door geometry.

Pro-tip: Located in Binh Lam village within Tuy Phuoc district (about 22 km from Quy Nhon center), easily reached via local provincial roads.
