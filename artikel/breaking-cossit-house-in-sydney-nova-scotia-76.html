Standing in the historic North End of Sydney, Nova Scotia, Cossit House is the city's oldest surviving residence and one of the oldest buildings on Cape Breton Island. Built in 1787, the house has witnessed more than two centuries of history, and according to local ghost stories, some of its earliest residents may never have left.

According to recent reports, the house was built for Reverend Ranna Cossit, a Loyalist Anglican minister who served as the garrison chaplain for the colony of Cape Breton Island. As the first Protestant minister assigned to the colony, Cossit played an important role in its early political and social development. He lived in the house with his wife, Thankful, and their growing family. Of the couple's 13 children, six were born within its walls.

Over the years, Cossit House has gained a reputation for paranormal activity. Staff have reported smelling unexplained pipe smoke in areas associated with Reverend Cossit, while visitors have described encounters they believe involve the spirit of his wife, Thankful, who died in the house during childbirth. Paranormal investigators and mediums have also visited the property, reporting unusual sounds and sensations.

Today, Cossit House has been carefully restored to reflect life between 1787 and 1800 and operates as a living history museum. Costumed interpreters demonstrate everyday activities of the late 18th century, including stitching samplers and churning butter, offering visitors a glimpse into domestic life in early colonial Cape Breton.

Whether the reported spirits are real or simply part of the house's enduring folklore, Cossit House remains a fascinating reminder of Sydney's early history, with a few ghostly stories lingering within its walls.

Recent findings suggest that cossit House Museum is located at 75 Charlotte Street in Sydney's historic North End, Cape Breton, Nova Scotia. The museum operates seasonally, generally from early June to mid-October.
