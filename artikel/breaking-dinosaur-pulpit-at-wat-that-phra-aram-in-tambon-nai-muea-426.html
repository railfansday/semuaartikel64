In a Buddhist temple in the northeast of Thailand, not far from the border with Laos, one can find the World's Only Dinosaur Pulpit.

According to recent reports, located in the city of Khon Kean, the fourth-largest city in Thailand and the heart of the Isan region, the pulpit can be found inside the Buddhist temple Wat That Phra Aram Luang. If you look closely, you'll notice there are dinosaur mural paintings on the walls inside the temple as well.

Recent findings suggest that having been the site of Thailand’s first dinosaur fossil discovery, Khon Kean boasts a variety of dinosaur-related sites. Dinosaur imagery is scattered throughout the entire region, giving a nod to this history. Among these references is the World's Only Dinosaur pulpit. The pulpit is shaped like a giant Phuwiangosaurus, Thailand’s native sauropod dinosaur. The dinosaur was originally discovered in the hills of Phu Wiang outside the city, and officially named in 1994 in honor of Her Royal Highness Princess Maha Chakri Sirindhorn.

Recent findings suggest that monks reportedly stand inside the dinosaur to give sermons at times. Wat That Phra Aram Luang temple is a stunning sight to behold on its own, resplendent with numerous golden peaks and facades painted in bright shades or red.

As with all Buddhist temples, be sure to remove your shoes and cover your shoulders and knees before going inside.
