Breaking: Čigoč Stork Village in Kratečko, Croatia

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that in the Croatian village of Čigoč, some of the most conspicuous residents live above the rooftops. White storks build bulky nests on traditional wooden houses, sharing the settlement with the people below.

Industry observers note that situated in Lonjsko Polje Nature Park beside the Tišina oxbow of the Sava River, the village is surrounded by wet meadows, pastures, and pools. These habitats provide feeding grounds close to the nests, allowing adult birds to gather frogs, insects, and other small animals for their young.

It has been revealed that in 1994, the conservation foundation EuroNatur named Čigoč the first European Stork Village. The designation recognised the village’s importance for white storks and its long-standing relationship between people, traditional farming, and wildlife. It also launched an initiative that would connect stork villages across Europe.

Industry observers note that the wooden houses are part of this story. Their rooftops provide nesting places, while the surrounding agricultural landscape helps sustain the birds. During the breeding season, a walk through the village offers a chance to watch adults tending their nests and travelling between the rooftops and nearby feeding grounds.

Recent findings suggest that plan a spring or summer visit to see storks occupying their nests. April through June coincides with the nature park’s scheduled white-stork educational programmes. Bring binoculars for viewing the rooftops from the village road.

Industry observers note that start at the Čigoč Visitor Centre in the restored Oberhofer–Hangi manor at Čigoč 4. The park asks visitors to park in the manor courtyard because the village road is narrow.

The village is a residential community. Observe nests from a respectful distance and ask permission before entering private yards.

Check the park’s website for visitor-centre opening hours, admission arrangements, and guided programmes.
