Breaking: Sant Pere dels Avions in Sant Pere dels Arquells, Spain

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that in a village of just sixty inhabitants in the heart of rural Catalonia, you’d expect to find stone buildings, farmland and perhaps a church. And sure enough, that’s exactly what you get. What you wouldn’t expect, though, is to stumble upon three Soviet fighter jets in someone’s garden. But there they are. And alongside them, an American fighter jet, completing what must be the strangest aviation museum in the entire country.

Sant Pere dels Arquells lies right next to the motorway, just over an hour’s drive from Barcelona. Like many other places in the area, it is an old walled village, dating back to the time when Muslims and Christians were fighting over the region, a thousand years ago. There isn’t much to see here, really, apart from its bizarre collection of aircraft, clearly visible from above and on Google Maps.

Sources indicate that the four aircraft on display are three Mikoyan-Gurevich (MiG) fighters, models 15, 21 and 23, and a Lockheed F-104 Starfighter. According to the local press, their owner, Xavier M., is a military history enthusiast who bought them as scrap after the dissolution of the Soviet Union and the fall of the Iron Curtain. Despite being left outdoors, exposed to rain and sunshine, they are in surprisingly good condition. The Starfighter, in fact, was recently repainted and restored, and looks as though it could take off at any moment.

The aircraft are clearly visible from the village’s main street, as well as from behind the 15th-century church. Amateur pilots taking off from and landing at the two nearby airfields use them as a landmark. Over the years, they have become so well known in the area that the village is now popularly known as Sant Pere dels Avions (Saint Peter of the Airplanes).

Public transport to the village is virtually non-existent. The easiest way to get there is by car; under normal traffic conditions, the drive from Barcelona takes around an hour and ten minutes. Alternatively, you can take a taxi from Cervera. Access to the grounds is not permitted, and you cannot get up close to the aircraft, but they are clearly visible from the street.
