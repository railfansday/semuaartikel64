In the spring of 1860, the Democratic Party went to its national convention and came out of it as two parties.

Industry observers note that the presidential election that followed had four serious candidates, two of them Democrats, and it was really two separate races, one in the North and one in the South. Abraham Lincoln won it with 39.8 percent of the popular vote.

The Democrats met in Charleston, South Carolina, on April 23, 1860, and the fight was over slavery in the western territories. Southern delegates wanted the platform to endorse a federal slave code; Stephen Douglas, the Illinois senator who had beaten Lincoln for a Senate seat two years earlier, knew that would cost him every Northern state.
