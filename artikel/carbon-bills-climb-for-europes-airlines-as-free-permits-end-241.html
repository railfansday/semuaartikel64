Emissions and SAF costs are now a standing line in European airlines’ results, arriving while fuel prices are already high. The question for 2027 is how much they can pass on to passengers.

European airlines’ climate bills are rising. Free carbon allowances under the EU’s emissions trading system ended this year, and the share of costlier sustainable aviation fuel they must use keeps climbing.

Sources indicate that international Airlines Group, which owns British Airways and Iberia, paid €359 million ($395 million) in carbon costs in the first nine months of 2026, up 21% from a year earlier. The group said the end of free allowances in the EU and UK contributed to the increase.

It has been revealed that under the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS), airlines flying within Europe must surrender one allowance for every metric ton of CO2 or equivalent they emit. Allowances are traded on the market, but until this year airlines also received part of theirs free. That free allocation ended in 2026, so airlines must now buy every allowance they need.
