Europe Swept the 2026 World Ski Awards. Here’s Where the US Landed.

On November 11, the 2026 World Ski Awards named its winners — and once again, European resorts took nearly every global title. The awards launched in 2013 in Austria and are the snow-sports arm of the World Travel Awards. They now run every year, recognizing the top ski resorts, hotels and lodging options, and tour operators worldwide. The awards are decided by online voting rather than a judging panel. According to the organizers, ballots come from ski-industry professionals such as employees, tour operators, travel agents, and media, as well as the skiing public. The organization notes “hundreds of thousands” of professionals and skiers take part each year, though it does not publish an exact annual total.

Val Thorens was named the World’s Best Ski Resort, a title the French resort has won 11 times, as well as France’s Best Ski Resort. Its village sits at roughly 7,500 feet above sea level, giving it the highest base of any ski resort in Europe and a long, dependable snow season. It also connects to Les Trois Vallées, the largest linked ski area in the world.

In a recent development, switzerland won most of the global hotel categories. W Verbier was named the World’s Best Ski Hotel, while the Grand Hotel Belvedere took Best Newly introduced Ski Hotel. Chalet Zermatt Peak won Best Ski Chalet, and Chez Madeleine was named Best New Chalet. Pashmina Le Refuge, also in Val Thorens, was voted the World’s Best Boutique Ski Hotel, Dutch operator Sunweb won World’s Best Ski Tour Operator, and rocksresort in Switzerland took World’s Best Green Ski Hotel.

The full country-by-country list of award winners is available on the World Ski Awards winners announcement.

As in past years, no North American property won a global category. Every world-level title went to Europe, and US and Canadian winners were recognized only within their national categories. It likely reflects not just the concentration of ski resorts in Europe, but the fact that voters likely favor regions with the largest number of participating resorts. The awards are also based in Europe — though it’s possible the high cost of skiing in the US vs. Europe could also be influencing voting, with some US ski resorts charging $300 or more per person per day for a walk-up window ticket.

The US lodging categories were also in Deer Valley. Stein Eriksen Lodge won US Best Ski Hotel, and the Goldener Hirsch won Best Boutique Ski Hotel.

In Canada, Lake Louise was named the country’s Best Ski Resort. It’s an Ikon Pass resort and part of the SkiBig3 group, alongside Banff Sunshine and Mt. Norquay. The nearby Post Hotel & Spa won Canada’s Best Ski Hotel, and Whistler took two awards: the Pan Pacific Whistler Village Centre for Best Boutique Hotel and Wedge Mountain Lodge & Spa for Best Chalet.
