FAA Lifts 4-Hour Nationwide Ground Stop; Disruptions to Last Into Weekend

Recent findings suggest that this is only the third nationwide ground stop in 25 years. The last one, during a 2023 system outage, lasted about two hours.

The Federal Aviation Administration ended a nationwide ground stop Friday night more than four hours after a communication issue affected the Air Route Traffic Control Centers, the high-altitude air traffic control facilities that manage flights between airports.

According to recent reports, the ground stop was lifted at around 8:15 p.m. ET, but disruptions are expected to linger into the weekend.

In a recent development, as of Saturday morning, there were more than 4,000 flight cancellations, according to FlightAware. Airlines canceled over 9,000 flights on Friday.

During a televised briefing Friday night, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin noted there was no indication of a cyberattack.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy noted at 3:36 p.m. ET the FAA started "experiencing instability" at radar and communication at air traffic control centers and TRACONs (Terminal Radar Approach Controls). He said 3,500 planes were in the air at the time.
