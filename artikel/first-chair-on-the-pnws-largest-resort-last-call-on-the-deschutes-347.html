First Chair on the PNW’s Largest Resort, Last Call on the Deschutes: Why Bend Is the West’s Best Ski Town

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Snowbanks start lining the road a few miles past the last roundabout on Century Drive. It’s moderately dark out, and you have a piping hot coffee in your hand from Backporch Coffee Roasters. Ponderosas start thinning into hemlock as the road gains elevation. A few miles from the Mt. Bachelor parking lot, the 9,000-foot cone of the stratovolcano itself indicates up dead ahead. Some people call this view from this particular spot in the road the “hallelujah curve.” On a wintertime trip to Bend, this is when the stoke really sets in.

Mt. Bachelor is 22 miles from downtown Bend. Here, you can ski high-alpine bowls, old-growth tree runs, and as much corduroy as your legs will take. Pair that with the Pacific Northwest’s most vibrant apres-ski dining and brewery scenes and a notable lack of interstate highway traffic, and the winter experience here is as evergreen as the Central Cascades themselves.

Sources indicate that bend packs a lot into a small city. Winter is also the cheapest time to be here, which directly counters the cold weather wallet punch in most ski towns. Good thing you’re the one planning this winter’s ski trip. Follow this lead for the lowdown on why Bend should be your ski destination this winter.

Sources indicate that the mountain stats come down to one word, reliability. Bachelor’s elevation and volcanic shape give it a season that regularly runs into May. Last winter was lean across the West and Bachelor still skied better than most of the Northwest, with a base that held well into spring. Most ski towns go dark when the lifts stop. Bend has live music, fire pits, shopping, and theater, to pair with world-class dining.

A Bend ski day starts with where you sleep. >Trailhead Lodge offers the best price on Mt. Bachelor lift tickets you’ll find in town, and a shuttle that drops guests at the mountain and brings them back. The property has an onsite cafe and bar with a general store in case you forgot any essentials. Opt for a King Suite room and you’ll have a private jacuzzi and sauna. If you’re traveling with kids or with your crew, the Whole Crew bunk rooms sleep up to four across three beds.

It has been revealed that the dog-friendly property has a pool and spa and onsite gear storage so you’re not stuck leaving the skis in the car overnight. Outside there’s a fire pit area and plenty of space to lounge and plot the next day’s adventure. What separates Trailhead, though, is the shuttle – no scraping a windshield in the dark or hunting for parking, and importantly, nobody has to be the one who stops at one beer after the last run. If you’re up for a river stroll along the Deschutes after a day on the hill, the path is accessible right from your room.

If you’d rather drive, the trip up Century Drive is worth doing at least once. The road climbs west out of town through open pine until the trees thicken and Bachelor’s summit fills the windshield. It’s about 30 minutes in decent conditions, which is why you’ll see locals knocking out a few laps before work. The alpine start allows plenty of time to get ready at the base, with Cocoa’s Cafe open for breakfast and coffee.

Sources indicate that ride to the top of Bachelor and you can drop off the summit to anywhere the compass points. On a bluebird day, follow the sun around the summit as it softens each aspect in turn. On a storm day, get to the leeward side and into the trees, where it’s calmer and you can actually see.

The 360-degree layout also means the mountain skis like three different resorts. Up top it’s massive, open alpine terrain, on par with what you’d find in the Rockies or Europe. Lower down, off Sunrise and Cloudchaser, the slopes are mellow enough for beginners to get their legs. Off Northwest, the resort turns into a forest of old-growth hemlock, spaced Japow-style for optimal turning with soft snow that hangs around for days after a storm.

Ask a local where to start and you’ll get a long answer, especially if that local is sitting with one or more other locals. The condensed version:.

According to recent reports, the run that explains the mountain is: Take off from the summit to the base of the Cloudchaser chair in one 2,700-vertical-foot push, high alpine to deep trees. Your legs will be on fire by the bottom. For the best view, head over to the top of Northwest, where the Three Sisters (volcanic peaks lined up in a row) rise up across the horizon. For trees, stay on Northwest or drop off Leeway into Ed’s Garden. Keep in mind that the tree wells around those hemlocks can be a real hazard, so ski with a partner and keep eyes on each other.

Recent findings suggest that beginners should find Marshmallow, which is as gentle as the name suggests. Intermediates have more options than they can use, but Wanoga and Old Skyliner are good places to start. Experts head for the bowl off the summit and drop through what’s known as “the keyhole,” as steep and committing as Bachelor gets. And if you just want to carve on groomers, head to Kangaroo.

If you’d rather earn it, skin laps on the Cone, an in-bounds side-country area, with a free uphill permit, or make the short hike from the top of the Summit chair to the true summit for the steepest lines into the bowl.

It has been revealed that by mid-afternoon your legs are cooked and you’re back in Bend with half a day left. Start at the Old Mill District, where the old smokestacks that once powered the mills still stand over shops and restaurants along the Deschutes. Walk the river path, get a drink by the water and browse for the gear you didn’t need. If your quads are filing a complaint, this is the slot for a soak or a sauna (more on that below).

Recent findings suggest that bend’s beer reputation hits with the legends. There are more than 30 breweries and taprooms in and around town, and the Bend Ale Trail strings a lot of them into a self-guided route that doubles as a solid après blueprint. Deschutes Brewery and Bend Brewing are among the most well-known, but more under-the-radar places like Terranaut, Funky Fauna, and Bevel are racking up the awards, including UPP Liquids, twice named best brewery of its size in the country.

According to recent reports, happy hours run late afternoon across town and plenty of patios keep heaters and fire pits going all winter. Grab a seat outside at Crux Fermentation Project as the sun drops and you’ll likely catch the last light on Bachelor, where you can pretend to see the line you took earlier.

According to recent reports, zydeco Kitchen & Cocktails puts Southern flare on dishes including halibut and steaks, with classics including a Shrimp and Crawfish Jambalaya and Blackened Redfish. Pine Tavern Restaurant is the city’s long-running staple for burgers and northwest rockfish.

According to recent reports, the convivial atmosphere at The Podski, a food cart pod in the city center, makes for a popular gathering space for locals and visitors. Dining options span the globe and the craft beer flows, well, like craft beer tends flow in mountain towns. After that there’s live music throughout town and a dance floor if you have anything left. If you don’t, a quick Uber ride  back to Trailhead is waiting, and first chair can come early.

It has been revealed that the closest airport is Redmond Municipal (RDM), about 25 minutes from Bend. Plenty of people drive in from Portland. Bachelor’s season generally runs early December into May. Midwinter brings the deepest snow, though spring brings sun and a silky “corn snow” cycle that’s second only to bottomless powder skiing. Either way, winter hotel rates are the best Bend sees all year.

It has been revealed that even committed skiers need a down day. Most of what follows works just as well as an afternoon add-on after a half day on the hill. Start with a 200-degree sweat at Gather Sauna House followed by a dip in the river just steps away. Bend has a deep bench of soaking pools, saunas and full-service spas, plus a few hot springs within driving distance for anyone willing to make a day of it, and a sauna session followed by a cold plunge does more for ski legs than another beer. Then there’s snow without skis. Snowshoe trails leave from the sno-parks along Century Drive, and guided bonfire tours wind through the forest after dark and end around a fire with something warm in hand. With Oregon Trail of Dreams, you ride behind a dog team through the Cascades backcountry. Ice skating and snowmobiling run through the winter too, the kind of thing that sounds secondary until you’re under a blanket watching the snow pile up.
