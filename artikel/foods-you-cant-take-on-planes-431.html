Foods you can’t take on planes

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

One of the best parts about travel is all of the fresh food and drink you’re exposed to. Sometimes, you find a food you like so much that you want to take it home with you. Depending on what that food or drink is, though, it might not make it past the TSA.

Every traveler has become accustomed to throwing away bottles of water, soda, and Pedialyte (there’s nothing worse than an in-flight hangover) before going through airport security. There are some liquids with different rules, however, and some banned foods that might surprise you. The TSA has a simple rule: “If it’s a solid item, then it can go through a checkpoint. However, if you can spill it, spread it, spray it, pump it or pour it, and it’s larger than 3.4 ounces, then it should go in a checked bag.”.

That’s the 3-1-1 rule, and it’s still in place in 2026: Each liquid has to be in a container of 3.4 ounces or less, and all of them have to fit in one quart-size bag. At airports with newer CT scanners, you may get to leave that bag in your carry-on. The Department of Homeland Security has also said it’s reviewing the rule, but for now, the limits haven’t changed.

Sources indicate that if it’s not on this list, then it’s good to fly in carry-ons or checked bags, although TSA agents may ask you to take it out so they can take a look at it. These are the TSA food rules to know before you go.

Recent findings suggest that allowances: You’re allowed to bring alcohol in containers of 3.4 ounces or less (think minis) in your carry-on bag, as long as they all fit in your quart-size bag. For checked bags, you can take up to 5 liters (1.3 gallons) per person of anything between 24 and 70 percent ABV, so long as it’s in unopened retail packaging. In checked bags, anything 24 percent ABV or lower has no limit.

Prohibitions: You can’t take anything higher than 70 percent ABV in checked or carry-on bags. And even if you carry minis on, you can’t drink them on the plane.

Allowances: Formula, breast milk, juice, toddler drinks, and baby food (including puree pouches) are allowed in checked and carry-on baggage, even over 3.4 ounces, and they don’t need to go in your quart-size bag. You don’t even need to be traveling with your child to bring breast milk or formula.

Prohibitions: The only stipulations are that the amount in carry-on bags is limited to “reasonable quantities” and that you tell the TSA officer about them at the start of screening.

Allowances: All solid cheese is allowed in carry-on and checked bags, but you might be asked to take it out of your carry-on. Creamy cheese is allowed without restrictions in checked bags.

Prohibitions: You’re only allowed creamy cheese in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on bag.

Allowances: Solid chocolate is allowed in carry-ons and checked bags. Liquid chocolate is allowed without restrictions in checked bags.

Prohibitions: You’re only allowed liquid chocolate in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on bag.

Allowances: Feel free to take as many dips and spreads as you can eat in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: You can only take creamy dips and spreads in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: Completely frozen ice and ice packs are allowed in carry-on and checked bags, and so are frozen gel ice packs. Dry ice is allowed in both with your airline’s approval, up to 5.5 pounds, in a vented package marked as dry ice.

In a recent development, prohibitions: The TSA specifies that any ice that’s even partially melted or slushy (or, you know, water) has to follow the 3.4-ounce rule in your carry-on. Melted gel ice packs get the same treatment, unless they’re medically necessary or keeping breast milk or formula cold.

Allowances: Flying home from Thanksgiving? Bring all the leftover gravy you want in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: You can only take gravy in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: There’s no limit on the amount of honey you can bring in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: You can only take honey in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: Bring all the hummus you want in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: You can only take hummus in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: You can take ice cream in your checked bag. In your carry-on, stick to containers of 3.4 ounces or less, so single-serve cups, not pints.

It has been revealed that prohibitions: Got a pint? Eat it before security, because it’s not making it through. Liquid ice cream is allowed in checked bags, but you might want to just get latest ice cream at that point.

Allowances: Bring all the jam and jelly you can handle in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: You can only take jam or jelly in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Recent findings suggest that allowances: The TSA is actually very OK with you bringing your live pet lobster through security. It’s even OK with live lobsters that aren’t your pet that you initiative on eating later.

In a recent development, prohibitions: Your lobster has to travel in a clear, plastic, spill-proof container, and a TSA officer will look it over. Checked bags are fine. For carry-ons, check with your airline first.

Allowances: Bring all the maple syrup you want in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: You can only take maple syrup in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: Bring all the oils and vinegars you can handle in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: You can only take oils or vinegars in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: Any amount of peanut butter (and all other nut butters, for that matter) is good in checked bags.

Prohibitions: You can only take peanut butter in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: No limit to the amount you can bring in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: You can only take salad dressing, salsa, and sauces in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: Bring all the soup you want in your checked bag.

According to recent reports, prohibitions: To the TSA, soup is just as much a liquid as your bottle of water. You can only take soup in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: Bring all the yogurt you want in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: You can only take yogurt in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: Meat, seafood, and frozen food are allowed in carry-ons and checked bags. Stack up your checked bag with as much as you’d like to pay for.

Prohibitions: Any ice or ice packs keeping your seafood, meat, and frozen foods cold in your carry-on must be completely frozen when you go through security. Partially melted with a bit of water at the bottom? Those ice packs won’t make it through. Dry ice (see No. 6) is the safer bet.

Allowances: If you want to turn your checked bag into a weight, go right ahead.

Prohibitions: Cans can look unusual on the X-ray, which could lead to extra screening that ultimately leaves the can with the TSA agent. And anything packed in liquid, like canned fruit or vegetables, falls under the 3.4-ounce rule anyway. Better to check it or chuck it.

Industry observers note that allowances: None. This one stays home no matter which bag you pack.

Sources indicate that prohibitions: Cooking spray isn’t allowed in carry-on or checked bags. Pick up a new can when you land.

Allowances: Homemade or store-bought, bring all you want in your checked bag.

Prohibitions: Cranberry sauce counts as a spread and eggnog counts as a liquid, so you can only take them in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: Bottled juice, cold brew, and other drinks are fine in your checked bag. You can also bring an empty thermos or water bottle through security and fill it up on the other side.

Prohibitions: You can only take drinks in containers of 3.4 ounces or less in your carry-on.

Allowances: Dry and “moist” pet food count as solid food, so they’re fine in carry-on and checked bags.

Prohibitions: Wet pet food has to follow the 3.4-ounce rule in your carry-on, and there’s no exemption for prescription pet food, even for service animals.

Allowances: Powders are allowed in carry-on and checked bags.

Prohibitions: Anything over 12 ounces has to go in its own bin at screening and may need extra screening, including being opened. If you don’t need it on the plane, pack it in your checked bag.

Recent findings suggest that even foods that aren’t banned can get pulled for extra screening if they look odd on the X-ray, and the TSA officer always has the final say. When in doubt, look it up first with the “What Can I Bring?” tool on tsa.gov or in the MyTSA app. If it still seems iffy, check it, ship it, or leave it at home.
