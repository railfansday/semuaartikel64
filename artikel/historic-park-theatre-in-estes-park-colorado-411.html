Historic Park Theatre in Estes Park, Colorado

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that the Historic Park Theatre’s most famous feature was born from heartbreak. Its tower, added by owner Ralph Gwynn in 1929, is known locally as the Tower of Love — not for the romance it celebrates but for the one that fell apart. Legend has it that Gwynn was left at the altar, and he built the tower as a tribute to the woman who broke his heart. Decades later, when a new Estes Park sign ordnance threatened the glittering neon that outlines it, residents took the matter to a ballot and won the theater a variance. So the Tower of Love kept its glow.

Construction on the theater itself began in 1913. The architect Joseph R. Anderson designed the building in the Classical Revival style, with parts of it fabricated in the city of Longmont and hauled up to Estes Park for assembly.

It has been revealed that gwynn, a projectionist from Denver, bought the theater in 1922 at the height of the silent-film era and ran it until his death in 1963. He added the lobby in his first year, the tower in 1929, and the neon lights in 1938.

Recent findings suggest that mickey and Ola Stanger began running the theater in 1967, initially on a lease from businessman Vic Walker before eventually buying the building. What started as a two-person operation eventually became a family affair, stretching across multiple generations, with a Stanger granddaughter, Jenna MacGregor, running it today. The theater was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1984.

Industry observers note that today the Park Theatre operates year-round, screening classics, documentaries, independent films, and first-run blockbusters alongside live music and other events. The concessions offerings have recently been updated, and adult visitors are encouraged to enjoy their movies with a cold beer. The projection technology has also come a long way from Gwynn’s era: A digital projector, Dolby 3-D, and Dolby 5.1 surround sound arrived in 2013, a new screen in 2015, and new auditorium seating in 2024. A historic designation, it turns out, doesn’t require you to sit in the original seats.
