📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Looming over the quiet countryside of An Nhon, Hoang De Citadel represents an extraordinary historical palimpsest where two distinct Vietnamese empires converged upon a single plot of land.

Originally built in the eleventh century as Vijaya, the formidable stone-walled capital of the Champa Kingdom, the grounds were transformed in 1776 when Emperor Nguyen Nhac established it as the imperial stronghold of the Tay Son movement.

Beneath its current overgrown earthen ramparts lie layers of complex history: medieval Cham elephant sculptures and carved stone guardians stand alongside ruined Tay Son palace foundations and later Nguyen Dynasty tombs.

Walking through its weathered stone gates reveals an unsettling, atmospheric blend of ancient spiritual art and military strategy, offering a profound glimpse into a fortress that witnessed the rise and fall of rival dynasties.

Accessible throughout the day, though early morning or late afternoon provides a moody, shadow-filled atmosphere ideal for exploring the scattered ruins and carved stone relics.

Located in An Nhon town, easily reached via national highways heading north from Quy Nhon center.
