Hockanum School House in Hadley, Massachusetts

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that built in 1840, this one-room School House served as the educational center for all of the first to eighth grade children in the Hockanum area, later serving only up to sixth grade. This was one of several school houses serving small neighborhood communities around Hadley.

A 1647 Massachusetts law required all towns with 50 or more families to hire a teacher to instruct their children in reading and writing. Parents and students contributed to their school in the form of money, goods, supplies, and chores.

According to recent reports, these were one-room schoolhouses, on land that was usually donated. Most schools had one book, the "New England Primer," that was used to teach the alphabet, syllables, and prayer.

It has been revealed that the last class to go through this school was in 1933. By then, it was more economical to bus students to the center of town than to keep the neighborhood school houses open.

Along with the outhouse next to it, the Hockanum School House was, and still is, taken care of by the Hockanum community.
