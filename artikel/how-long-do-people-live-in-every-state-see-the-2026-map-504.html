Sources indicate that there are 343,035,829...30, 31, 32 people in the U.S., and counting. Someone is born every nine seconds, and someone passes away every 10 seconds.

Recent findings suggest that it might not seem like it as we go about our daily lives, but the state you live in can actually affect how long you live. Some areas offer greater access to healthcare, more parks and green spaces, healthier food options, and living conditions that support longer lives, while others don't.

Before we dive into life expectancies in each state, recorded by World Population Review, let's look at the highest and lowest, along with the national average.

The state with the highest life expectancy in the U.S. is Hawaii. Residents live to just over 81 years old on average, likely thanks to a combination of factors: weather, lifestyle, and environmental conditions that foster longevity. According to The Hill, Hawaii has very low rates of cancer and heart disease. Residents also have better access to healthcare, and the air is cleaner than in many other states.
