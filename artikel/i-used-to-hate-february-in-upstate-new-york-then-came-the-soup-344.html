Sources indicate that “Come on downnn! Roll and spin!” My teeth chattered and breath puffed into the air as I searched for the source of the announcement from my spot halfway down the line snaking down the Phila Street sidewalk. Rounding the corner, my eyes landed on a jovial gentleman clad in a purple suit and yellow-tinted aviators; he’s really in character. A few minutes later, and it was our turn to roll.

My partner’s dice hit the green felt with an emphatic thwack. “That’s a seven! It’s that easy, my friends,” yelled the purple-suited host. “You get a free chowder,” the dealer exclaimed from behind a mock roulette wheel and a table riddled with giant playing cards, poker chips, dice, and uniquely, steaming crockpots. Most people flock to my hometown city of Saratoga Springs for the summertime horse races, natural springs, or, in winter, as a gateway to snowy Adirondack cabins. But I brave February’s biting cold most years for one thing: soup.

According to recent reports, the setting couldn’t be further from a casino, and my gamble wasn’t for money—it was for chowder. Along with about 20,000 other attendees bundled in parkas and wool hats, I was in my hometown of Saratoga Springs to judge soup at my favorite annual festival: Chowderfest.

It has been revealed that we were in front of Solevo Restaurant in downtown ‘Toga to taste our first sample of the day: clams casino chowder. My hands rejoiced at the warmth from a small cup of New England-style broth swimming with garlic-butter littleneck clams, bacon, peppers, and breadcrumbs. Straight out of the gate, this one was the one to beat for me; the rich flavor, ample clams, and contrast of the breadcrumbs came together for a chef’s kiss.

In a recent development, as a gateway hub to the massive Adirondack Park’s eastern region, Saratoga isn’t short on visitors, especially in summer when the track opens. But despite the crowded streets, Chowderfest is different. It’s mostly locals, out in the cold on the slowest weekend of the year, eating soup on the sidewalk. It’s our mid-winter beacon: a city-wide party paying homage to gastronomic creativity through chowder in all forms. This year marked the event’s 27th year, and my 11th time attending, with 88 restaurants and vendors slinging chowder of all kinds.

It has been revealed that clams are optional, and thinking outside the box is highly encouraged. I’ve had a Southwest shrimp and corn chowder, and Druthers Brewing Company has won People’s Choice in the festival’s top sales tier every year since 2023. Past winners have included a red Reuben chowder and a ravioli chowder. This year’s entry was a smoky corn chowder amped up with a fancy garnish of bacon crumbles and a lobster hush puppy.

According to recent reports, february gets a bad rap in Upstate NY. Winter kicks into full gear as already-towering snowbanks creep higher, icicles dangle like giant pointy fingers from roofs, and the temperatures dip into the negatives more days than not. I’d always despised February growing up—I wanted spring to show up already. But shortly after graduating college in Vermont and moving back home, that changed. On the first Saturday of February 2005, I discovered Chowderfest in all its glory: downtown closed to traffic, a community showing up to mingle and support local restaurants during the slow season, and happy visitors ready to party…hard. Live music wafted from numerous watering holes, throngs of people embraced the bitter cold to celebrate chowder, and despite my toes going numb, I loved every minute of it. I’d just moved back to my hometown and wasn’t sure I wanted to be there. That afternoon was the first time Saratoga felt like a place I’d choose, not one I’d ended up in.

Since then, I’ve lived in Manhattan and Italy before eventually circling back to Upstate. Whenever possible, I’ve made a point to attend Chowderfest, even braving the slippery streets on crutches one year after breaking two toes on my left foot the night before. But nobody hobbles through a February crowd on crutches for the soup alone, and I went for the same reason I went in 2005: the whole town out on the street in the cold, propping up its restaurants through the slowest stretch of winter and having a very good time doing it. I’ve brought my sister, numerous friends from out of town, and my partner, all to share the unique soup-fête experience over the years.

For me, Chowderfest has fostered a deeper appreciation of soup, particularly when I travel. Soup is universally comforting, feeds the masses, salutes local ingredients, and is typically one of the most affordable meals. Now, I seek out local soups as part of my culinary and cultural connection to a destination: pumpkin soup in Slovenia, fish soup in Iceland, quinoa soup in Peru—all wonderful. The bowl that inspired a return trip to the far-flung Faroe Islands came with good conversation. In a local’s home, cozy and shielded from the wind that had been whipping me in the face all day, I tucked into what has become the soup by which I measure all soups: a creamy, almost curry-like fish soup with potatoes and root veggies. I had two bowls while chatting with the homeowner’s son-in-law, who told me about his favorite place in the archipelago — which I then visited on my next trip four years later.

With so many participating restaurants, it’s impossible to try every single chowder. This year, I hit up some favorite joints like Cantina, which dishes out a chiles en nogada (chiles in walnut sauce) chowder inspired by a classic Mexican dish made with roasted poblano peppers, picadillo beef, roasted pork, dried fruits, and a creamy pepita sauce. It’s heavy on the meat and wonderfully creative. I also popped into some newbies: a fresh bar on Caroline Street (the party street) called Lucy’s, where staff were ladling out a cheddar cheeseburger chowder that got bonus points from me, thanks to the tater tot toppers.

Recent findings suggest that despite the cold, I took my gloves off to vote. As predicted, clams casino from Solevo was my number one chowder this year, and I’ve already marked the date in my calendar for the next one. For someone who spent her childhood wanting to skip February, it’s an odd month to plan a year around. Saratoga’s summers are mostly for tourists, but for one weekend in February, the town throws a party for itself, and that’s the weekend that really makes me feel at home.

According to recent reports, the festival takes place annually on the first Saturday of February. The upcoming 2027 event will be on February 6 from 11 AM to 4 PM, but the party downtown keeps going all night, with a slew of live music events and food and drink specials. The festival is free to attend, with four-ounce chowder samples available to purchase for $2 each. Everyone is a judge, and attendees can easily vote in the app (found in the App Store or on Google Play) for their favorite chowder.
