Sources indicate that amadeus gained distribution share and built a bigger airline IT business. Sabre and Travelport remain weighed down by heavy debt and a heavier reliance on distribution.

For decades, the companies that controlled airline distribution occupied one of the most vital positions in the travel industry. But making money from travel bookings and building a profitable technology business around them have turned out to be very different propositions. The contrasting fortunes of Amadeus, Sabre, and Travelport show just how different.

In 2000, Amadeus accounted for an estimated 26% of global GDS air bookings, compared with 34% for Sabre, including Abacus. Galileo and Worldspan, then separate systems that were later combined into Travelport, together had about 40%. By 2023, the estimates were 47% for Amadeus, 32% for Sabre, and 21% for Travelport, according to Redburn Atlantic data.
