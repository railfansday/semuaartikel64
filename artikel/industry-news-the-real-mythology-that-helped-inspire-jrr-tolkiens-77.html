Recent findings suggest that tolkien read Old Norse, and the most famous description of elves in it comes from the Prose Edda, written by the Icelandic chieftain Snorri Sturluson in the 13th century. Snorri describes a heavenly place called Alfheim and splits the elves in two: "The light-elves are fairer than the sun to look upon, but the dark-elves are blacker than pitch," and the dark ones live down in the earth.

Industry observers note that tolkien gave his own elves a split along the same line: in his mythology, the elves who made the journey west and saw the light of the Two Trees of Valinor became the Calaquendi, or Elves of the Light, and the ones who never got there were the Moriquendi, the Elves of Darkness. The Tolkien scholar Tom Shippey has argued that this division was Tolkien's way of making sense of Snorri's, with history doing the sorting instead of skin color.
