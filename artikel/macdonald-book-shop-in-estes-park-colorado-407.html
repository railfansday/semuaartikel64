At Macdonald Book Shop, a cozy and beloved store in the Colorado mountain town of Estes Park, selling books began as a side hustle.

J. Edward Macdonald bought a small log cabin in Estes Park in 1908 and opened a hardware and general store next door — with one corner full of books for sale. When he retired, his wife Jessica turned the parlor of their home into a proper bookstore, also selling magazines, stationery, and school supplies. That was 1928. Books have been the focus ever since.

Over the decades, the shop expanded to claim more of the original house. Four generations of Macdonald women kept it going. After J. Edward died in 1932, Jessica ran the store on her own until her death in 1957. Ownership passed to their daughter Louise, and when Louise died in 1971, her daughter Paula Steige took over. Steige retired from teaching in 1976 and reopened the shop year-round — it had been a summer-only operation.

In a recent development, then, in July 1982, the Lawn Lake Dam failed. Four feet of water tore through the bookstore, destroying two-thirds of the inventory. But the community indicated up to help: More than 100 volunteers helped rebuild the foundation, rip out and replace the floor joists, paint, clean, and shelve upcoming stock. The store reopened 18 days later. Steige later called it “a blessing in disguise” — the old house needed renovation anyway.

The Macdonald name is still on the door, though ownership changed again in 2020, when Anastacia Galloway Reed and Kevin Reed, who also own the neighboring Inkwell & Brew, bought the shop.

The bookstore still operates in the same cabin on Elkhorn Avenue, still stocks around 15,000 titles, and still does what it’s done since Jessica first set up in the parlor: It gives people a place to find a book and a welcoming vibe to those who want to sit down and read.
