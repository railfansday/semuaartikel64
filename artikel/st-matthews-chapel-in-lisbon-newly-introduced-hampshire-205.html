St Matthews Chapel is an historic, darling, and well maintained church building located in the picturesque White Mountains of New Hampshire. Built in 1893 (1883 by some accounts - but the cornerstone lists 1893), it is mentioned to be one of the most photographed churches in New England. On first sight, the delighted traveler will know why it is so widely photographed. Surrounded by vibrant foliage in the fall, it offers a serene and meditative landscape. It is just as beautiful in the early summer, when it’s surrounded by fields of blooming purple lupine. The church itself is a stunning example of the Gothic Revival style that was popular at the time of its construction. Its pastoral charm is evident from its vibrant yellow door to its detailed stained glass depicting mountain scenes. One notable stained glass piece, located near the baptismal font,  is said to be from Tiffany.

Recent findings suggest that originally a chapel that served the wealthy vacationers that flocked to the area in the late 1800's and early 1900's, the chapel now serves as an Episcopal Summer Church. They do still hold services here on Sundays, but only in July and August, and the first Sunday of September. Their website states that visitors are welcome. You don't have to be religious to appreciate this breathtaking scene where both the church and the surroundings are equally striking.

As a side note, this chapel is available to people of all faiths (or no formal faith) from May til September/October  for weddings, baptisms, memorial services and other such events.

Also nearby, be sure to check out The Frost Place and maybe grab some breakfast at  Polly’s Pancake Parlor.

Because this is a seasonal chapel, they are served by visiting pastors through out the summer, and have no live-in clergy or daily operational staff. Therefore, to view the interior of the chapel, you have a few options:.

Doors are open to the public May-October on Sundays starting at 10am (when services are held). Visitors of all backgrounds are welcome to attend services, or come tour the interior of the chapel following services.

Sources indicate that the chapel is occasionally open outside of Sunday services for weddings, baptisms, or other scheduled community events, and the interior can be viewed during these times (if appropriate to the event taking place). Call ahead to find out what, if any events are scheduled during the time of visiting.

Lastly, you can explore the exterior grounds which are completely open to the public 24/7 and easily accessible and visible from the main road. An exploration of the grounds and landscape make this stop worth the time all by itself.
