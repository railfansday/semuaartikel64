Europe leads with half the countries in the top 10, but the highest-ranked country is from somewhere else.

Recent findings suggest that in South Africa, 32.3% of internet users over the age of 16 have used a gambling or betting website sometime in a month timeline, leading all countries. Online platforms, for example sports betting apps, have quickly grown in the country in the past few years, contributing to the rise. For the 2023-24 fiscal year, online betting was more than 60% of gambling revenue for the country, beating out traditional casinos.

Industry observers note that norway came in a close second with 31.2% of internet customers gambling in one month of data. The country has a high rate despite having tight restrictions and laws, including mandatory loss limits and monitoring players for harmful behaviors. Greece came in third place with 25.8%, well below the percentage set by South Africa and Norway.

You may be surprised to learn that the United States isn't in the top 10 countries with the highest rates of online gambling. After all, online gambling has taken over television ads and sports sponsorships all over the country.

According to recent reports, despite that, the United States only ranks 38th. Data from GWI found that around 10.2% of Americans over the age of 16 who have used the internet in the past month have gambled online. That puts the country between Poland at 10.4% and Italy and 10.1%.

Industry observers note that even with just slightly more than 10% of internet owners over 16 years old gambling online, the market is still a major revenue generator in the country. In 2025, the market size of online gambling was more than $31.8 billion, with the industry expected to increase to around $50 billion by the year 2030.

Online gamblers are more likely to be men than women. A 2024 survey by Fairleigh Dickinson University found that 15% of men mentioned they had taken part in online gambling before while only 6% of women had participated.
