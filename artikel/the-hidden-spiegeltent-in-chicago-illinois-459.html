The Hidden Spiegeltent in Chicago, Illinois

🌐 Source: Atlasobscura • 📅 25 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (342 words, 📖 Standard)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Spiegeltents were made to travel. These round wooden dance halls, lined with mirrors and stained glass, went from fair to fair. They went up fast and came down fast. The one on Randolph Street is not going anywhere. It was built indoors, on the 14th floor of a Loop building.

The building opened in 1926. Rapp & Rapp designed it, with the Oriental Theatre movie palace on the lower floors. The theater is now the James M. Nederlander Theatre. The whole building stands on the site of the Iroquois Theatre, where a fire in 1903 became one of the deadliest in American history.

High above the theater, the architects put a large hall for Masonic meetings. It sat empty for decades. Later it was turned into offices, and a dropped ceiling hid the room completely. When the building was converted into the Cambria Hotel, workers took that ceiling down and found the old hall again, steel bow trusses and all.

In 2019 the tent came over from Belgium in pieces, about 4,000 of them. Belgian tent builders put it together inside the hall, the traditional way, with no nails. It is about 72 feet across. From the hall, it looks like a small circus parked under factory trusses. Inside, there is carved wood, stained glass, velvet and mirrors everywhere. The mirrors are not just for looks. They let guests see around the posts that hold up the roof.

Since October 2026, the tent has been the home of Kermess by Circensia, which kept it right at the center of the room.

The tent is on Floor 14 of the Cambria Hotel Chicago Loop, at 32 W Randolph St. Go in through the hotel and take the elevator up. There are no walk-in visits and no tours. The only way in is with a ticket, booked ahead through Kermess. The current show, Premonitions Inc., is for guests 21 and over. Right next door are the Nederlander Theatre and Couch Place, the alley tied to the 1903 Iroquois Theatre fire.
