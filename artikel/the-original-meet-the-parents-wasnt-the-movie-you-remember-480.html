However, this movie was not actually an original production: it was a remake of the 1992 indie film of the same title, directed by the filmmaker Greg Glienna. The original is also a much darker film than its more famous remake: as Variety put it, "Who ever heard of a comedy where everyone gets maimed, drowned, shot or commits suicide?... 'Meet the Parents' turns a visit with the in-laws into a living nightmare." Here's a look at the original film on which a long-running franchise was built (and which the distributor later tried to keep the public from seeing).

According to recent reports, like the later version of the story, the 1992 film explores the idea of what happens when a man meets his future in-laws, but in Glienna's version, it ventures into much more serious territory, with multiple characters being killed as the story unfolds. Unlike the expensive Hollywood remake, the original was shot on a budget of only $30,000, and in addition to directing, Glienna played the lead role and co-wrote the script with Mary Ruth Clark. The project was originally sold to National Lampoon to be released as part of their self-titled series of films, but the deal fell through, leaving its future uncertain at first. Â.

Industry observers note that however, it received some positive critical attention when it made its debut: Variety reviewed the film when it premiered at the 1992 Just for Laughs Comedy Festival in Montreal, and described it as a "wonderfully twisted dark comedy." Their review also gives a number of details about the little-seen movie's story, including their summary of the plot's framing device: "Presented almost entirely in flashback, the tale begins when a gas station attendant warns a client en route to meet the future in-laws to 'turn back' while he still can. Ultimately, Universal acquired the rights to the film with the intention to make a new version based on the same premise.

However, after Glienna sold the remake rights to Universal, it turned out there was a catch in his deal. In a 2024 interview, Glienna explained: "I got a lousy lawyer, and we signed a contract. I don't know why this wasn't explained to me, or I didn't comprehend it, but I could only release it after a certain amount of time in a not-for-profit situation. I don't know why I would have agreed to that. Now, anytime I've tried to get the rights to release the film, they say no. I don't know why they don't want me to release this film.".

Glienna expressed his disappointment at the hurdle which had been placed in front of his original version. "We made this film to be seen. Why would they not let us show [it to] people?
