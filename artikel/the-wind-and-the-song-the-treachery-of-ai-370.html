The wind and the song: the treachery of AI

🌐 Source: Aeon • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (153 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that from finding human figures in the stars to creating prehistoric cave art, we’ve been anthropomorphising our world for millennia. Now, machine-learning technologies can generate photorealistic images, synthetic voices that laugh and pause for breath, and chatbots that crush the Turing test. We’re seeing the first generation in history for whom the line between humans and their representations could all but vanish.

In this video essay for the digital publication Fully Human, the UK animator and filmmaker Lewis Heriz interrogates where this is taking us. We inevitably build our world through symbols, he says, but the hall-of-mirrors reality we’re rapidly creating may be leading us down a perilous path. However, he argues, we’re not without agency. Instead of submitting to a world where data and people are indistinguishable, we should be thinking critically and imaginatively about how we might use AI in ways that preserve our humanity.
