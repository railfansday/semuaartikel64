Thermes Romains de Cimiez in Nice, France

The Roman baths of Cimiez originally belonged to the ancient Roman city of Cemenelum, located in the modern district of Cimiez, Nice.

Recent findings suggest that the ruins of the frigidarium of the northern baths have always been visible, never having been covered over since Roman times. The frigidarium of the northern baths was long mistakenly called the "Temple of Apollo".

According to recent reports, three thermal complexes were eventually uncovered; the north, east and west complexes. The three groups follow the same layout, with the axis and the order of the rooms are identical.

The ancient city's aqueducts brought water to the thermal complex. The comprehensive water drainage system consists of a complex network of sewers and canals which is still visible around the site.

At the beginning of the 5th century, the first bishop of Cimiez, Saint Valerian, established the episcopal complex here made ip of a cathedral, baptistery, outbuildings, and the bishop's residence located north of the baths. The basilica was built on the Roman walls using reused materials. The columns from the northern baths were used in the construction of the baptistery.

According to recent reports, only a tiny part of the ancient city of Cemenelum has been excavated so far. As such there is still debate about the exact dates of the bath complexes.

Access to the site is via the museum.
