This Huge US Ski Resort Has the World’s Most Reliable Snow, Upcoming Data Claims

Sources indicate that in early November, the 2026 World Ski Awards handed out its winners, with only a few nods for ski resorts in North America. But a second ranking just came out, and while it doesn’t rate the best ski resorts, it rates something that may matter more to die-hard skiers: which resorts have the most reliable snowfall.

It has been revealed that the ranking comes from Inghams, a British travel company that has sold packaged ski trips since 1934. It ranked “reliable snow” — not just how much snow it gets, but how dependably lifts and runs stay open from one season to the next — at major ski resorts and regions using historical data rather than reputation. And the resort at the top is decidedly not in the Alps. It’s not even in Colorado or the Rockies. The distinction goes to Mammoth Mountain in California, which scored 93.9 out of 100 on what the company calls its “Reliable Ski Score.”.

In a recent development, sprawling Niseko United in Japan took the second-place spot. Photo: Various images/Shutterstock.

Many “best of” ski lists come down to a basic metric, like how much snow a resort gets on average or how many people vote for it online. But the Inghams process is more involved, scoring 133 resorts and regions on five weighted factors, then averaging them to get a score out of 100. The largest share (35 percent) was based on “mountain running,” or the average percentage of lifts and slopes open during peak season. Snow adequacy, defined as the share of core-season days with at least 30 centimeters (about a foot) at the base, accounted for another 25 percent. The rest of the score rewarded consistency from one season to the next (20 percent); downside protection, or how far a resort’s conditions drop in its worst season compared with a typical one (12 percent); and the average depth of the snowpack on the mountain, where deeper simply scores higher (8 percent).

The researchers pulled seven winters of data, from 2017/18 through 2025/26 (removing the two COVID-affected seasons), and defined peak season as January 5 to March 20. The figures were compiled from public historical listings on skiresort.com and are current as of September 2026.

Mammoth’s win is thanks to its high elevation (the Main Lodge base is about 9,000 feet above sea level) and its long seasons, which can stretch from October into June in strong years. It was the only resort to score well across all five of the reliability measures at once, which is what separated it from the pack.

Photo: Visit Mammoth/Patrick Griley.

Below Mammoth, the rest of the top picks are spread across four countries: Niseko United in Japan (93.3), Pontedilegno-Tonale in Italy (93.1), Obertauern in Austria (92.9), and Jackson Hole in Wyoming (92.5).

Industry observers note that the biggest surprise is how much of the list sits outside the Alps. North American resorts took eight of the top 20 spots. Canada landed five, giving it more than any single European country except France, and the US had three resorts in the top 10. Canada’s Banff Sunshine had one of the most impressive numbers in the study: it hit the “snow-adequacy” bar on every single peak-season day across all seven winters, making it the only resort to get a perfect 100 percent. It’s not a huge minimum, requiring only about a foot at the base, but meeting it every day for seven years is something no other resort managed.

France was the strongest European performer, with five resorts in the top 20, including Les 3 Vallées, the largest linked ski area in the world.

There is a commercial angle here, as there usually is with operator research. Inghams sells packaged trips to many of the resorts it scored, and it backs those bookings with a Snow Guarantee that will move travelers to a nearby resort, free of charge, if fewer than a quarter of the mountain is open due to lack of snow. So in some ways, the ranking doubles as a sales pitch for the idea of booking reliability in the first place.

Industry observers note that nonetheless, the research is data-driven, and the winning mountains are worth a look if you’re deciding where to plan your ski trip this season. The World Ski Awards reward the resorts skiers love, but this list rewards the ones most likely to deliver on conditions, and the two lists aren’t always the same.
